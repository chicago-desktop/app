                          ABOUT THE SOUNDS
                          ================

There are none.

This desktop lives in a terminal, and a terminal has exactly one sound: the
bell, which most people switched off in 1994 and never switched on again.
There is no wave device, no MIDI synthesiser and no sound scheme, and the
system does not pretend otherwise - SYSTEM.INI says sound.drv=(none) and
means it.

What is missing, for those who remember:

  THE STARTUP SOUND       Six seconds, composed on a Macintosh, and the only
                          part of a mid-nineties desktop that people can
                          still hum thirty years later.

  TADA.WAV                Two seconds of triumph, attached by default to
                          nothing in particular, and therefore played mostly
                          by people testing their speakers.

  CHIMES.WAV              The polite one. Played when something had gone
                          wrong but not badly enough to deserve CHORD.WAV.

  CHORD.WAV               The other one.

  CANYON.MID              Two and a half minutes of MIDI canyon, shipped
                          with the system, opened by everybody exactly once,
                          and remembered by everybody forever.

  THE MODEM               Not a system sound at all, but the sound of a
                          machine connecting to anything: a dial tone, eleven
                          digits, a hiss, a second hiss answering it, and
                          then silence that meant it had worked. See
                          Program Files\Online Services\DIALUP.TXT.

The files in this folder are the names, kept as names. Nothing will open
them, and that is correct: a program that opened CANYON.MID and played it
silently would be worse than the silence.
